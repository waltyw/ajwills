Lasting Powers of Attorney

Make sure people you trust can step in and make decisions on your behalf, exactly as you would want — should you ever need them to.

Protect Your Future While You Still Have the Choice

What is an LPA

A Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) allows you to choose someone you trust to make decisions on your behalf if you become unable to make them yourself.

Many people believe their spouse or children can automatically make decisions for them — but this is not the case. Without an LPA, your loved ones may need to apply to the Court of Protection, which can be costly, time-consuming and stressful.

Putting LPAs in place gives you peace of mind that your wishes will be respected by the people you have chosen.

Why It Matters

Why LPAs Matter

Anyone can lose mental capacity unexpectedly — through illness, an accident, or as part of aging. An LPA ensures the right people can act quickly when it counts.

Immediate Authority

Your chosen attorneys can act without delay, avoiding costly and stressful Court of Protection applications.

Your Choice of Attorney

You decide exactly who is trusted to make decisions on your behalf — not the court.

Peace of Mind

Knowing your wishes will be respected, in exactly the way you'd want, if you're ever unable to speak for yourself.

✿
Two Types

Types of LPA

There are two separate types of LPA, and we recommend most people put both in place.

Property & Financial Affairs

This LPA allows your chosen attorneys to help manage matters such as:

  • Paying household bills
  • Managing bank accounts
  • Buying or selling property
  • Dealing with pensions and investments
  • Managing your finances if you lose capacity

This LPA can also be used with your permission while you still have mental capacity, if you choose.

Health & Welfare

This LPA allows your attorneys to make decisions about:

  • Medical treatment
  • Care arrangements
  • Where you live
  • Daily care and wellbeing
  • Life-sustaining treatment (if you choose)

This LPA can only be used if you no longer have the mental capacity to make these decisions yourself.

Plan Ahead

Benefits of Early Planning

An LPA can only be set up while you still have mental capacity — so the earlier it's in place, the better protected you and your family will be.

  • Registration can take several months, so early action avoids gaps in protection
  • Removes the need for costly, stressful Court of Protection applications later
  • Gives you full control over who is chosen, and what authority they have
  • Provides reassurance for the whole family, not just yourself
