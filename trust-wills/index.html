Trust Wills

A Trust Will places some or all of your estate into a trust, rather than passing it directly to beneficiaries — giving you more control over how and when your assets are used, long after you're gone.

As we're based in Buckingham, we serve clients across Buckinghamshire and Oxfordshire through home visits and remote video or phone consultations — whichever suits you best. Trust Wills are commonly used to protect assets for young children or grandchildren, provide for a vulnerable or disabled beneficiary, protect against future care home fees, or provide for children from a previous relationship in a blended family.

We'll take the time to understand your family's circumstances and explain, in plain English, whether a trust structure is right for you and which type would work best — with fixed, transparent fees agreed upfront.

  • Protect assets for children and grandchildren until a suitable age
  • Provide for vulnerable or disabled beneficiaries
  • Support blended family arrangements
  • Help with Inheritance Tax and care fee planning where appropriate

Questions Answered

Frequently Asked Questions

A Trust Will is a Will that places some or all of your estate into a trust, rather than passing it directly to beneficiaries. A trust holds and manages assets on behalf of the people you want to benefit, under conditions you set out in advance.
Trust Wills are commonly used to protect assets for young children or grandchildren until they reach a suitable age, provide for a vulnerable or disabled beneficiary without affecting their means-tested benefits, protect against future care home fees, or provide for children from a previous relationship in a blended family.
Common options include a life interest trust (giving someone the right to benefit from an asset during their lifetime, with the asset passing to others afterwards) and a discretionary trust (giving your trustees flexibility over how and when beneficiaries receive assets). We'll help you decide which structure, if any, suits your circumstances.
Trust Wills involve more detailed drafting than a straightforward Will, so fees are typically higher. See our Pricing page for clear, fixed fees confirmed in writing before any chargeable work begins.
The trustees you name in your Will manage the trust on behalf of your beneficiaries, following the terms you've set out. We can talk through who might be suitable and what the role involves.
Often, yes. A trust can allow a surviving partner to benefit from a home or savings during their lifetime, while still guaranteeing that children from a previous relationship ultimately inherit — something a standard Will cannot always achieve.
